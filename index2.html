Project Near Copies Date October, 2026

One Prompt Many Answers

About

Near Copies collects what an image model gives back when you ask it for the same picture again and again. We kept each prompt, changed one small detail at a time, and saved every answer: a hand that moves, a strand of hair that falls, light that shifts by an hour.

Side by side, the results look less like errors and more like contact sheets from a single shoot. This page lays them out the way a designer might, in rows, stacks and grids, and lets each arrangement turn into the next as you scroll.

Seven answers to one prompt about balance: the same few shapes, stacked a different way each time.

Stacked once more, the seven variations balance like the shapes inside them.

The Prompt

Every image on this page began as a sentence. Sent once, it returns one picture. Sent again with a single word changed, it returns a cousin of that picture, close enough to compare and different enough to notice. The small gaps between those versions are what this project is about.

Twenty scenes in one style, printed in two inks, again and again.

Keeping a Face

Asking a model for something new is easy. Asking it to keep something the same is harder. To hold a face, a sweater or a horizon steady across six images, each one was made from the one before it. What stays fixed says as much about the model as what changes.

Sixteen ways to draw a circle

Series or Alternatives

In a grid, near copies read like frames from one roll of film. In a stack, they read like a flipbook. Cut and joined, they turn back into a single picture. The images below never change. Only their order, size and spacing do, and that alone decides what you see first.

Six takes on one portrait. The orange moves, the man stays, and the pile keeps every version.

Cut into bands, six variations add up to one face. Almost, but not quite.

Same pose, same sky, six moments apart. Arranged by size, they start to read like a sequence.

Whose Hand

Ask for a pig painted in the manner of a famous artist and you can fill a wall with them, every one plausibly from the same brush. The style is borrowed, the subject is absurd, and the question it leaves behind is the one this collection keeps coming back to.

Who is the artist?

Same Pieces

Abstract compositions make the pattern easy to see. The same spheres, discs and thin red frames return in every image, only rearranged. Dealt out into a grid, eight compositions look like one box of building blocks played with eight times.

Same pieces, new order

What Comes Next

Image models keep getting better at doing exactly what they are told. The near misses may become rarer, which is a good reason to keep them now. This page is a small archive of them, and an excuse to try new ways of laying images out.
